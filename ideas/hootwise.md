# Hootwise (formerly "The Owl"): council briefing

> Renamed by the principal on 2026-11-01, after the council. The debate below ran under the working name "The Owl" and is kept as it was. Hootwise is still subject to the clearance check the council set: UK IPO (classes 9, 16, 41, 42), domain and both app stores.

**Rounds:** 3, because the principal asked for three.

**Packet:** partial company default. It included the product inventory (Fair-Ring, Rekindl, the festival platform) and org facts: a solo founder with a full-time job and an agent-run organisation. **The constitution was not included**, because the `christoe-os` repo could not be attached this session. The persona definitions came from the copies in this Claude project, not from the repo. Nothing from `ideas/` or `decisions/` was used.

**Idea as councilled:** a wise, grandfatherly English-voiced owl narrates values and STEM stories for ages 1–13. The channels are stories, social scenarios, how-tos, Ask Owl, Cookery Owl and careers. Parent-voice cloning is a parked later phase. "The Wise Hour" was floated as a name.

---

## Moat

- **Named:** Victoria opened with three mechanisms:
  - (a) switching cost from the child's progress and the parent's recorded voice
  - (b) a dataset of children's Ask Owl questions
  - (c) owned character IP
- **What survived:**
  - (a) was conceded. Retention is unproven, and the voice feature is parked and easy to re-record elsewhere.
  - (b) was conceded. Children's free-text data is a Children's Code liability before it is an asset.
  - (c) is the only mechanism left. Victoria narrowed it to "a byproduct of the pilot to protect, not a plan to fund". Sally accepts it is the right bet but says it is "a bet, not a moat" until children ask for the character by name unprompted.
- **Status:** no proven moat. There is one conditional mechanism, character IP, and its test has been agreed.

## What all four agreed

- **Narrow the launch:** one age band, ages 4–8 at launch. Ages 1–13 is at most a one-page "register ladder" in a thin character bible.
- **Defer channels:** Ask Owl, Cookery Owl, careers and parent-voice cloning are design constraints to keep open, not v1 scope. A v1 with no child accounts and no free-text input avoids most of the Children's Code, DPIA and COPPA load.
- **Content is the critical path, not code.** A sustainable pace is about one story every 1–2 weeks: 5 for a pilot, about 30 for a one-band launch library.
- **Test before building:** run a no-app pilot of five stories with 5–10 families over 2–3 weekends at near-zero spend. Use a human or premium voice for story one, so a weak voice doesn't read as a weak concept.
- **Family lifespan is about 2–3 years,** so price and plan for that, not a "10-year relationship".
- **Names:**
  - "The Owl" is a placeholder only.
  - "The Wise Hour" is a tagline at most, not a mark.
  - Name the brand and the character separately.
  - File one mark, the brand, and only after clearance.

## Unresolved forks

1. **What the pilot must prove.**
   - Victoria: the key signal is a child asking for the owl by name, unprompted, in weeks 2–3, with a neutral-voice control family.
   - Sally and Anton: that is a child signal, and the child doesn't pay. The pilot must also show parent behaviour: unprompted reorders, activity completion, and a real price ask such as "Story 6 costs £2, want it?"
   - Sally and Anton also want controls for sample bias, because friends are polite. Their fix is to include 3–4 families once removed, sent the link with no personal introduction.
   - *Settles it:* writing the pass/fail bar before the pilot starts.
2. **Capacity against Fair-Ring and Rekindl.**
   - Paul: there are about 6–8 usable hours a week. A third product takes 25–40% of them and everything crawls. Only one should be active at a time, and the pilot should be time-boxed.
   - No one argued it fits alongside the others.
   - *Settles it:* the principal's priority order across the three products.
3. **Acquisition and economics.**
   - Sally: a 2–3-year lifespan means continually finding the next cohort of 4-year-olds, with no obvious channel for a part-time solo founder.
   - Sally also raised that annual plans or story packs may beat a monthly subscription.
   - Victoria: younger siblings re-enter, and the brand can follow the family. She frames this as licensing upside, not a reason to build now.
   - *Settles it:* naming one acquisition channel and testing a price point in the pilot.
4. **Market.**
   - Sally: the careers example ("70% in your matric") implies South Africa, but the packet assumes the UK.
   - *Settles it:* the principal choosing the launch market.

## Sharpest risk and sharpest opportunity

- **Risk (Sally):** "Who pays, and for what outcome?" Today a parent would lose almost nothing by leaving, and this is the most crowded, lowest-willingness-to-pay corner of children's media.
- **Opportunity (Victoria, narrowed):** the grandfather voice combined with make-something-real activities. That pairing, not "owl", is the differentiator. If children ask for the character by name, it becomes the one asset a catalogue can't copy.

**Light web check (orchestrator, not a trademark search):**
- No existing "Hootwise" app or brand surfaced. Searches for "hoot" are dominated by Hootsuite, and there is an existing children's app called *Hoot – My Story Maker*. That is the nearest collision for any "Hoot"-led brand in kids' stories.
- No "Grandpa Hoot" brand surfaced. There is a self-published book called *Old Hootie Owl*.
- "The Wise Hour" didn't surface as a product, but "wise"-named podcasts are common.
- *Hoot and Peep* is a Penguin picture-book series.

A UK IPO and EUIPO search is still needed.
