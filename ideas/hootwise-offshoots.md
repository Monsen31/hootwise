# Hootwise offshoots: council briefing

**Rounds:** 2, under the default policy because no round count was given. No third round was run. After Round 2 all four personas converged on the order and differed mainly on whether the loop they described is real, which is an empirical question a test settles and debate can't. Round 2 was the closing round.

**Packet:** partial company default. It held the product inventory (Fair-Ring, Rekindl, the festival platform, Hootwise as an idea), org facts (a solo founder with a full-time job) and a short description of the Hootwise core idea. The constitution was **not** included, because the `christoe-os` repo couldn't be attached. Persona definitions came from this Claude project's copies. The earlier Hootwise briefing and anything else in `ideas/` or `decisions/` were **not** given to the council.

**Idea as councilled:** four offshoots around Hootwise:
1. YouTube Shorts
2. "The Wise Hour" storytelling (form left open)
3. A website with videos and activities
4. Games (added by the principal at the start)

---

## Moat

- **Named (Victoria, Round 1):** an owned content library plus a first-party parent email and engagement dataset. She argued each story produces four assets, and each asset feeds an owned audience that shows what retains children. Her case was a distribution-and-data loop, with Shorts at the top.
- **Attack (Sally):** three links in the loop are unevidenced:
  - **Reach:** about 50 Shorts a year from an unknown brand.
  - **Capture:** a time-poor parent leaving a feed to hand over an email.
  - **Learning:** a few hundred subscribers give anecdotes, not a dataset.
- **What survived:** Victoria conceded that no offshoot is a moat on its own. Shorts and games are rented distribution, the site has no network effect, and character and voice are brand, not moat. She now holds a narrowed claim: a shared library plus a **parent-only** email list, with no child data. She says it compounds *only if* the sequence is built to capture it, and is otherwise "four distractions", Sally's phrase, which she accepted.
- **Status:** the narrowed mechanism is on the table but untested. Sally treats it as a hypothesis. Victoria agrees it can only be settled by a test.

## What all four agreed (after debate)

- **Audio first.** An audio-only Wise Hour is the cheapest offshoot, about 2–4 hours an episode. It is the only one that is screen-free and fits the brand promise, and the only true reuse of a script. Paul and Victoria now treat it as **the first product, not an offshoot**. It can ship before any app, and it doesn't need the character's visual design.
- **No live or in-person Wise Hour for now.** That is a different business, with safeguarding, DBS checks, a venue and insurance.
- **Lock the character and voice first.** They are the dependency for every format, and until they exist each format risks rework.
- **Cadence is the binding constraint.** At about 5–8 hours a week, one weekly output is sustainable.
- **Shorts: no child-aimed Shorts.** If done at all, they would be parent-facing and an experiment, not a pillar.
- **Games last.** Games come after the story shows retention. They only fit the brand if they are finite, with no streaks, ads or return rewards, and lead off-screen ("now go build it").
- **Website premature.** At 25–40 hours it is affordable, but there's nothing above it in the funnel yet.
- **Parent data only.** Any data is parent-only, collected with consent, with no child data, which keeps it within the Children's Code.

## Unresolved forks

1. **Does free audio convert, or just satisfy?**
   - Anton: a free podcast is *enough* for a parent, and that is the problem. Parents pay only for what it can't give: the full ad-free library, offline play, the matching printable activity, and later a parent-voice option.
   - Victoria: free audio plus email capture is the start of the loop.
   - *Settles it:* a few free episodes plus an optional printable behind a sign-up, measuring sign-ups and requests for more.
2. **Is "content atomisation" real?**
   - Victoria (Round 1): one story yields a Short, an episode, a web page and a game level at close to the cost of one story.
   - Paul, using his own estimates: script about 3h, audio about +2h, Short 4–6h, web page plus activity 5–8h, game level 15–30h for the first. That is about 8–10x the story, so only audio is true reuse.
   - Victoria narrowed her claim to audio-first in Round 2 but didn't directly concede the numbers.
   - *Settles it:* timing the first real units.
3. **Shorts now or later?**
   - Sally would allow a time-boxed test of 8–10 parent-facing Shorts with a stop condition and measured click-through.
   - Paul: no hours on Shorts or web until the audio test passes.
   - *Settles it:* the order the principal sets.
4. **Does the voice hold for 20 minutes?**
   - Sally and Paul both flag whether a synthetic grandfatherly voice survives a full episode, and whether commercial TTS licensing allows it.
   - *Settles it:* one episode played to a real parent, plus a check of the TTS licence terms.

## The test on the table (Paul, endorsed in substance by Sally and Anton)

- **Scope:** six audio Wise Hour episodes, 5–8 minutes each, about 30h over 6–8 weeks (about 4h a week).
- **Delivery:** plain RSS feed, no visuals, no YouTube.
- **Who:** 10 parents get the first three episodes.
- **Kill criteria:** fewer than 6 replay one unprompted on a second night, or fewer than 3 ask for more.
- **Then:** public reach is a second gate.
- **Before publishing:** check TTS licensing and the "Hootwise" mark.

## Sharpest risk and sharpest opportunity

- **Risk (Sally and Anton):** parents may not choose an unknown owl over free alternatives (Bluey, CBeebies, sleep-story podcasts, YouTube) at all. Every extra format spreads thin trust across more surfaces while the story itself is unproven.
- **Opportunity (Anton, with Victoria):** Hootwise could own **the 20-minute bedtime wind-down**, a screen-free, calm, shared routine. The rainy-weekend activity is a second, weaker moment. Audio is the cheapest way to that moment, and it is also what the app would need anyway.

## Questions left for you

1. Do you treat audio as the first Hootwise product, ahead of any app?
2. Will you run the 6-episode, 10-parent test, and accept its kill criteria as written?
3. Do Shorts, the website and games wait until that test passes, or does any one get a time-boxed slot now?
4. Is the free podcast the product, or a trust-builder for a paid tier? If paid, what goes behind the paywall?
